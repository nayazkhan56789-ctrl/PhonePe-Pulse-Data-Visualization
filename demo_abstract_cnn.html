BACKGROUND (87%)
Hypertension is a leading risk factor for cardiovascular disease worldwide.

BACKGROUND (74%)
Lifestyle interventions may reduce blood pressure but long-term evidence is limited.

OBJECTIVE (79%)
The aim of this study was to evaluate whether a 12-week supervised walking programme lowers systolic blood pressure in adults with mild hypertension.

METHODS (98%)
A total of 120 adults aged 40 to 65 years were randomly assigned to a walking group or a control group.

METHODS (100%)
Blood pressure was measured at baseline and every 4 weeks using a validated automatic monitor.

RESULTS (97%)
At 12 weeks, systolic blood pressure fell by 9.4 mmHg in the walking group compared with 2.1 mmHg in controls (p < 0.001).

RESULTS (98%)
No serious adverse events were reported in either group.

CONCLUSIONS (96%)
A supervised walking programme significantly reduced blood pressure in adults with mild hypertension and may be a useful adjunct to standard care.
